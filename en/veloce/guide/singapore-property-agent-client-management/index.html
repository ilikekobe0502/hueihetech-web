Property Agent Client Management in Singapore

Updated: 2026-11-02 · Hueihe Tech

Property agents in Singapore manage four kinds of clients at once: buyers, sellers, landlords and tenants. The simplest system is one profile per client tagged by role, with their requirements written down in a fixed format (HDB or condo, district or town, nearest MRT, size in sqft, number of bedrooms, budget and timing), a follow-up reminder after every viewing, and a dated reminder well before each tenancy ends. Handle personal data in line with the PDPA and follow CEA rules and your agency's policies at all times.

Why property agents lose leads

A typical week mixes new enquiries from listing portals, viewings, negotiations and landlord calls. Requirements arrive in WhatsApp messages and voice calls, and by the time a suitable unit comes up, nobody remembers who wanted a three-bedroom near the Circle Line under S$1.8 million. Leads are rarely lost to a competitor on day one. They fade because the follow-up never happened.

Four client types, four different needs

Client typeWhat to recordKey follow-ups
BuyerHDB or condo (or landed), towns or districts, nearest MRT, size in sqft, bedrooms, budget, financing status, move-in timingAfter each viewing; when a matching unit is listed; when budget or timing changes
SellerProperty details, expected price, reason and timeline for selling, next home plansRegular updates on enquiries and viewings; market check-ins
LandlordUnit details, asking rent, preferred tenant profile, current lease end dateWell before the lease ends; renewal or re-letting decision
TenantAreas, MRT line, budget per month, bedrooms, move-in date, lease length, pets or other needsAfter viewings; before their lease ends; when they may be ready to buy

One person can play several roles over time. A tenant today may be a buyer in two years, and a seller is often also a buyer. Tag each client by role and update it as things change.

HDB versus condo requirements

Write requirements in the same order every time so you can compare clients quickly:

  • HDB: town and estate, flat type (for example 4-room or 5-room), floor level preference, distance to MRT and schools, remaining lease the buyer is comfortable with, budget, and any eligibility conditions the buyer has already checked with HDB.
  • Condo: district, specific developments of interest, size in sqft, bedrooms, facilities that matter, freehold or leasehold preference, budget.
  • Both: nearest MRT station or line, timing, who else is involved in the decision (spouse, parents), and what would make them say no.

Leave eligibility and financing advice to the right parties. Note what the client has confirmed, not your own assumptions.

Viewing follow-ups that convert

  1. Write a note the same day. What they liked, what put them off, and how it compared with other units.
  2. Follow up within a day or two. Refer to something specific: "You liked the unblocked view but felt the kitchen was small."
  3. Refine the brief. Each viewing teaches you something. Update the requirements so the next suggestion is closer.
  4. Set the next reminder. Never end a follow-up without a date for the next one.

Lease expiry reminders

Tenancy end dates are some of the most predictable opportunities in the business. For each tenancy you handled, set a reminder a couple of months before the end date for both the landlord and the tenant. The landlord may want to renew or re-let; the tenant may want to renew, move, or start thinking about buying. Reaching out early, before they browse portals, keeps you in the conversation.

CEA rules and PDPA

This is general information, not legal advice. Property agents in Singapore must follow the rules set by the Council for Estate Agencies (CEA) and their agency's policies, including how they advertise and how they deal with clients. Personal data of buyers, sellers, landlords and tenants is covered by the PDPA, which generally requires consent for collecting and using it, limits use to stated purposes, and expects you to protect it. Marketing calls and messages to Singapore numbers also have Do Not Call rules. When in doubt, check with your agency.

FAQ

What is the best way for a property agent to track client requirements?
Use one profile per client tagged by role (buyer, seller, landlord, tenant) and record requirements in a fixed order: property type, area or district, nearest MRT, size in sqft, bedrooms, budget and timing. Update it after every viewing.
How soon should I follow up after a viewing?
Ideally within a day or two, while the viewing is fresh. Mention a specific thing they liked or disliked and suggest a next step, then set a reminder for the following follow-up.
When should I remind landlords and tenants about lease expiry?
A couple of months before the tenancy ends is a common starting point, so both sides have time to decide on renewal, re-letting or moving. Set the reminder as soon as the lease is signed.
Do property agents in Singapore need to follow the PDPA?
In general, yes. Personal data collected from clients is covered by the PDPA, and agents must also follow CEA rules and their agency’s policies. This is not legal advice; check with your agency or official guidance for details.
